Tips
  • Pair iPhone → Car via Bluetooth first, ideally as a speaker / media device, not just “phone call”. Then open this page and toggle Noise ON.
  • In the car, a good starting point is around -30 to -22 dB, then adjust by feel.
  • With Auto adapt ON, the app:
    • Keeps the masker near the level you chose with the slider,
    • Pushes it a bit louder when the cabin is louder, and a bit quieter when the cabin is quieter (within about ±8 dB around your slider),
    • And tilts between Brown / Pink / Gray to smooth out low vs high imbalance.
  • With Auto adapt OFF, the color and level are exactly what you choose (manual mode).
  • Add this page to your Home Screen (Share → Add to Home Screen) to make it feel more like a native app.
What am I seeing? (and what the code is doing)
  • Pink noise: Energy decreases slowly at higher frequencies (more energy in bass/mids, less in treble). This sounds smooth and natural and is a great all-around masker for most cars.
  • Brown noise: Much deeper and heavier. It has a lot of low-frequency energy (rumble region). Best for masking tire hum, road rumble, and wind buffeting, especially in louder cabins.
  • Gray noise: Shaped so all frequencies are perceived as similar loudness to the human ear. It feels “even” and airy. Good for quieter cars or EVs where the noise is more high-frequency than bassy.
  • Auto adapt (tone): When enabled, the app looks at the microphone spectrum: if the cabin is very bass-heavy (low bars much taller than high bars), it shifts toward Gray to add more highs; if highs dominate, it leans more Brown to fill lows; otherwise it tends toward Pink. This is a simple feedback loop to “flatten” the overall shape so the sound feels smoother and less fatiguing.
  • Auto adapt (level): The app measures overall cabin level (RMS in dBFS) and keeps the masker near your slider setting, nudging it gently: a bit louder when the cabin is loud, a bit quieter when the cabin is very quiet, but never more than about ±8 dB away from your chosen level.
  • Cabin (RMS): A rolling average of how strong the sound is at the iPhone mic, measured in dBFS (decibels relative to full scale). 0 dBFS would be a clipped signal, so normal car levels will be negative (for example −35 to −10 dBFS).
  • Spectrum plot: Each bar shows how much energy there is at different frequencies: low rumble on the left, speech and mid-range in the center, hiss/whine on the right.
  • FPS: Frames per second – how often the display and spectrum graph update. Values around 20–30 are normal; it doesn’t affect the audio, just how smooth the visual feels.
  • Voice-aware ducking: The code constantly listens to the mic’s mid-band energy (roughly 200–3000 Hz, where your voice lives). When it detects you talking, it smoothly reduces the masker level (up to about −12 dB) so passengers and phone calls can hear you more clearly.
  • Logging: Every ~0.25 s the code records a row with timestamp, RMS dB, whether noise is pink/brown/gray (manual), pink_auto/brown_auto/gray_auto (auto), or none when noise is off, plus 16 coarse spectrum bands.
